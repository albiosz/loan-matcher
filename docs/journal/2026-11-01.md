## Learned

- Business inputs behind the assumptions (not measured, from my own knowledge of the business):
  - About 3% of leads convert within 30 days (the generator's default; an earlier guess of 12% was too high).
  - People who need money are the ones who take personal loans, but a lead with good earnings who wants a loan will probably take it too.
  - Nearly any lead can convert, so the outcome is noisy.
- Resulting model of conversion: need raises intent, unaffordability lowers eligibility, so conversion peaks for low-to-middle disposable income; `dispo` and `debt_conversion` convert best, `journey` and `other` worst; probation and mini-job leads convert less.
- On the synthetic data a gradient boosting model reaches ROC AUC about 0.65 against about 0.69 for the noise-free signal. These scores only show the pipeline works and say nothing about real leads.
- Unit assumptions to check against real data: EUR per month, durations in months, `employment_duration = 0` means not employed.
- Comparing two datasets: Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic for numeric columns and total variation distance for categorical ones, both 0 to 1. Verdicts: below 0.10 similar, from 0.20 shifted (defaults, to be tuned on real data). Two synthetic datasets with different seeds score at most 0.01 on every column; income multiplied by 12 scores 0.91, so unit mismatches stand out.
- `.venv` had no matplotlib, scipy or scikit-learn; the first two are installed now, but the baseline model cell of `01_synthetic_data.ipynb` still needs scikit-learn.
